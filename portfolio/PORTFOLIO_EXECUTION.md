## Phase 1: Skydd evidence audit

### Central argument

Victory designed through Skydd's evolution from a focused insurance product into an eight-product protection platform across web, mobile, and marketing surfaces.

### Public facts currently verified

- The live product describes Skydd as evolving beyond insurance into one place to save, protect, and build wealth.
- The public launch presents eight product areas: GoalGuard, Future Scholar Fund, Income Shield, Auto Guard, Legacy Plan, Property Shield, Wellness Plan, and TravelGuard.
- The experience spans protection, savings, claims, policies, goal progress, and waitlist acquisition.
- Public trust signals include NAICOM regulation, Sterling Bank infrastructure, and more than NGN 1 billion in coverage issued.
- The current public experience includes a waitlist and product-interest selection across all eight products.

### Victory's ownership to evidence

### Proposed case-study structure

1. Hero: the platform expansion in one sentence
2. Context: old Skydd versus the new product model
3. Challenge: eight products, different rules, one coherent platform
4. Ownership: web app, landing page, waitlist, and mobile contribution
5. Architecture: how products, policies, claims, savings, and account management fit together
6. Selected flow: one end-to-end journey showing real product depth
7. System layer: components, states, naming, responsive behaviour, and handoff
8. Cross-surface launch: marketing to onboarding to product management
9. Results: only verified metrics and shipped evidence
10. Reflection: what changed and what Victory learned

### Public-safety labels

Every asset must be assigned one label before publication:

- Public: already live or explicitly approved
- Redact: usable after removing personal, financial, partner, or operational data
- Reconstruct: recreate with safe sample data
- Private: useful for reasoning but not publishable
- Permission needed: ownership or confidentiality is unclear

### Claims discipline

- State exactly what Victory owned and what she contributed to.
- Separate shipped, designed, proposed, and in-progress work.
- Do not imply that public company metrics are individual design outcomes.
- Use the verified waitlist figures only with their measurement source and date.
- Do not expose customer data, internal underwriting logic, commercial agreements, or confidential operational information.
