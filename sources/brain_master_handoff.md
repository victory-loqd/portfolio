# 7. LOQD — FULL CONTEXT

LOQD is Victory’s founder project and potentially the most distinctive piece of work in the portfolio.

It should not be reduced to:

“an app for storing things at events.”

That is not the interesting part.

## Core problem

People attending:

- concerts;
- raves;
- parties;
- nightlife events

often carry:

- bags;
- phones;
- power banks;
- clothes;
- valuables.

The problem is not simply that storage does not exist.

People already use workarounds:

- cars;
- friends;
- venue corners;
- carrying items all night;
- leaving belongings elsewhere;
- improvised storage.

The deeper problem is trust and custody.

## Research

LOQD has substantial primary-research material.

Research has explored:

- what people carry;
- what they lose;
- damage;
- inconvenience;
- current workarounds;
- willingness to pay;
- retrieval;
- trust;
- security;
- event behaviour;
- organiser needs;
- staff needs;
- failure scenarios.

## Central insight

The strongest design problem is:

> **How do you design a temporary-custody service that someone is willing to trust with their belongings in a chaotic event environment?**

That immediately leads to:

- check-in;
- storage;
- item verification;
- retrieval;
- delegated pickup;
- dead-phone fallback;
- no-internet fallback;
- high-value items;
- liability;
- staff access;
- incorrect release;
- theft;
- damage;
- insurance;
- capacity;
- throughput.

## Why LOQD matters strategically

It proves Victory can work before a product brief exists.

It shows:

- primary research;
- problem discovery;
- founder judgement;
- service design;
- business-model thinking;
- physical + digital systems;
- operational design;
- risk analysis;
- brand development;
- pilot planning;
- ability to revise assumptions.

## LOQD should be treated as a service, not an app

A key insight for presentation:

> The product is the service.

Software supports the service.

The portfolio should show:

**people + process + software + physical infrastructure + risk + communication.**

## Strong case-study direction

> **Designing trust into a real-world custody service.**

Alternative internal framing:

> **Designing for trust before designing the interface.**

## Important LOQD caution

Do not overclaim.

LOQD may have:

- strong research;
- organiser interest;
- pilot preparation;
- service design;
- operating logic.

That does not equal:

- product-market fit;
- validated scale;
- proven demand;
- proven unit economics.

Being explicit about the stage will increase credibility.

## Safety model

- circles;
- Family / Friends / Workers / Custom;
- consent-first invites;
- temporary sharing;
- always-on sharing;
- “until off” sharing;
- approximate location;
- paused states;

This should focus on:

- permission design;
- asymmetric relationships;
- temporary vs permanent access;
- precise vs approximate location;
- paused states;
- emergency states;
